Two mayors, one valley. You lead Hearth; the CPU leads Harbor. Both mayors try to turn effort into a thriving shared valley, but neither can see the other’s plan—only each new settlement and an imperfect public signal. Your effort scores combine above the board. Isolated districts still return to meadow, and every settlement needs breathing room.

Grow like a city—without rushing it. Every six turns the season changes. Hearth raises warm Italianate streets; Harbor builds cool Moroccan courtyards. Connected neighbors lay roads automatically. Individual houses and otherwise-alone neighboring pairs become farm homesteads; chains of same-community houses within two plots slowly join by rural roads after a season. But packing three or more newly settled houses directly together inside six turns creates a strained cluster: wellbeing drops sharply, growth pauses, and that mayor’s effort score takes a heavy temporary penalty. Extra houses and tighter blocks deepen the penalty until the early building rush ages out. Harbor understands this rule and will usually spread or pace its construction.

Follow the water. Wetlands and neighboring plots provide attractive water access, lifting wellbeing and growth. Spring rain can flood low ground; flooded farms produce less. Summer drought stresses dry inland land, while waterside settlements stay resilient. Blue cisterns mark settled plots with access. When two settled water plots share an unbroken wetland channel, cargo boats begin sailing between them; each weekly sailing moves six crates and gives the connected waterfront a wellbeing lift.

Complete the food chain. Settled fields and pastures harvest food each week. Farms route it through connected commerce: rural farming communes open farmers markets, while village centers and plazas with shops form marketing agencies that raise demand. Harvest becomes stock, then food moved through shops, markets, and deliveries.

Trade close to home. Shops serve people in their connected community. Walk-ins buy locally, while 35% of bicyclists become cycle couriers and carry up to four orders each. Farmers markets add rural trade; marketing agencies strengthen population-center commerce.

The weekly goods train. Every six turns mark a week. The freight train circles the whole valley and delivers two crates to every settled plot, no matter which community lives there. Trackside houses mature into depots after a season; when the train passes, each depot sends bonus goods through its connected rural-road community. Fresh rail service lifts wellbeing across the entire area for the new week; watch the timetable beside the year.

Wellbeing shapes growth. Quality of life rises with green land, roads, nearby shops, civic care, lively places, good weather, and manageable density. Healthy tiles gain more citizens; tiles above 58% mature each season, and tiles above 82% can grow twice. Struggling tiles pause their architectural growth. Flowering gardens mark thriving plots.

Care and culture emerge. Growing connected communities establish spiritual places first, then leafy parks, libraries, and finally hospitals. Mature neighborhoods can also open art studios, yoga studios, law offices, and botanicas. Along with libraries, parks, spiritual places, plazas, and playhouses, they generate cultural impact that adds directly to each mayor’s effort score. Italianate Hearth and Moroccan Harbor give every civic building its own architectural character.

The fault remembers density. Every third week, the valley’s fault shifts. The most tightly knit district of four or more directly connected plots is struck; extra internal street links deepen the damage. Its affected plots carry a massive temporary effort penalty for the following week, marked by cracked ground and rubble. Looser settlements escape the main shock.

Rare trades find their niche. Mature working land can attract grain mills, bakeries, smithies, and apothecaries. Larger centers may open coaching inns and playhouses; waterside communities develop fisheries and ferry houses. No two districts receive the same mix automatically—the land, age, population, and local connections all matter.

Build with the water. Wetland plots do not receive ordinary masonry. Hearth uses bright, buoyant timber houses inspired by the floating villages of Tonle Sap Lake; Harbor raises timber homes and verandahs on tall stilts, with pitched roofs and boardwalks inspired by Kampong Ayer.

Places emerge. Mature, small hillside clusters become villas. Five connected homes make a village center. Four or more homes become a farming commune when at least half sit on fields or pasture. A dense neighborhood of nine or more forms a paved plaza. Growth can change a place again as its shape and land use evolve.

Read the land. The twenty-by-twenty field holds 400 plots. Every new game raises fresh, steep-sided hills and deep hollows, then rerolls meadow, field, pasture, grove, wetland, and ridge. Working land gives a little extra seasonal growth. Empty regions held by one community score as territory.
